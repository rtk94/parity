## Notes and decisions (review these)

- **Age minimum set to 18, not 13+.** Recommended, because: (1) the Terms
  are themselves a contract, and contracts with minors are generally
  voidable, so 18 (age of majority) is the only floor under which the
  agreement is reliably enforceable; (2) allowing under-18 users pulls in
  COPPA (under-13 parental-consent rules) and GDPR's 13-to-16 age-of-consent
  variance by member state; (3) the app records debts between people, which
  is adult territory. Setting 18 is also consistent with the Privacy
  Policy's "not directed at children under 16" statement. If you want to
  lower it to 13+, you would need to add parental-consent handling and
  reconcile the two documents.
- **Load-bearing legalese, do not soften:** Section 7 (Disclaimer of
  warranties) and Section 8 (Limitation of liability). Under US law an
  implied-warranty disclaimer must be *conspicuous* (UCC 2-316) to be
  effective, which is why those sections use bold and clear headings rather
  than being buried in plain prose. Keep them visually prominent. The
  phrases "as is", "to the maximum extent permitted by applicable law", and
  the liability cap are the operative language; rewording them for
  friendliness weakens their effect. I used bold instead of ALL-CAPS, which
  is the modern convention and still satisfies conspicuousness, but do not
  demote them to normal body text.
- **Indemnification (Section 9) is optional.** You asked for a reasonable,
  non-enterprise limitation, so I kept the indemnity to a single short
  paragraph scoped to your own violations and misuse, not a broad
  enterprise-style clause. For a free/low-cost personal tool it is
  defensible to remove Section 9 entirely and rely on Sections 7 and 8. Your
  call.
- **This is not legal advice.** These Terms are a reasonable, plain-language
  starting point for a small solo-operated SaaS tool, but I am not a lawyer.
  Before relying on the liability, indemnity, and governing-law provisions,
  and especially before offering the service commercially, have them
  reviewed by a lawyer licensed in your jurisdiction.
